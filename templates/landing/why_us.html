We’re building assembly cells that learn from physical outcomes.

A1 brings the attempt, the outcome check and the preparation for the next attempt into one workcell, starting with connectors. The goal is fewer interventions, and less engineering effort each time a supported part variant changes.

A1 is in development.

Discuss your assembly task How the loop works, in detail

The expensive part is getting the next task ready.

A robot can repeat a motion all day. The time goes into the work around it: adapting the fixture, tuning the process, agreeing what counts as a good part, preparing material, recovering failed attempts and proving the next variant.

That surrounding work is what A1 is designed to shrink.

The acceptance test

Check the connection that matters.

A connector can look seated before it is locked. We are designing A1 around a physical acceptance procedure: an electrical continuity check and a mechanical engagement check, defined for the supported task.

Those checks are the evidence used to judge each assembly and to develop candidate skills. The cell is built around your definition of an acceptable part.

The reset

Make practice repeatable.

Learning takes more than another arm movement. The finished or failed assembly has to leave the station, and the next attempt has to start from a known condition.

A1’s planned carrier system advances prepared part pairs and routes every finished attempt to acceptance or quarantine, so practice continues between human interventions.

People still define the task, prepare and replenish parts, and handle exceptions. Each batch is finite.

The next variant

Make the next variant take less work.

The useful test of learning is what happens when the part changes. We are building toward reuse of motion skills, fixture knowledge and test methods across related connector variants.

We will measure the total engineering effort to qualify the next variant at comparable quality and throughput: fixture work, demonstrations, corrections, training, debugging, validation and interventions.

TargetVariant B qualified in under half the engineering hours of variant A, at matched quality. A target, not a result.

Practice, qualify, then run.

Candidate skills are developed in practice and evaluated on fresh parts against a fixed acceptance procedure. Production runs an approved skill version within defined limits.
